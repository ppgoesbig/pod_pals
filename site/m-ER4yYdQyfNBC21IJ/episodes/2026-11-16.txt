Okay, something you've been learning. Remember picking the memo's voice off that leaderboard? Here's what the numbers next to each voice actually mean, and why a gap of ten points is basically nothing.

An Elo score is really a prediction. The gap between two scores tells you how often one should beat the other. A gap of a hundred points means the higher one should win about two times out of three. A gap of four hundred means it should win about ten to one. And two voices with the same score are a coin flip.

Each vote nudges the scores. If the favorite wins, it barely moves, because that's what the math expected. If the underdog wins, it jumps, because the prediction was wrong. That's the whole engine. Surprises move scores, and expected results don't.

Here's the catch. With classic Elo, the order of the votes changes the final numbers. So most arenas don't run it one vote at a time. They take every vote at once and solve for the set of scores that best explains all of them together. Same idea, steadier answer.

And then there's the error bar. A voice with a few hundred votes might sit at a score give or take twenty or thirty points.
