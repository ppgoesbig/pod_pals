Okay, something you've been learning. Remember Gemini only handing back raw P.C.M., and the memo turning it into an M.P.3? You know P.C.M. is every sample, written down. Today's the other side. What M.P.3 actually throws away, and why you never hear it.

The trick is called masking. Your ears get swamped easily. A loud sound hides quieter sounds near it in pitch, and even for a split second right after it. So M.P.3 chops the audio into tiny slices, about a fortieth of a second each. For every slice, it works out what your ear can't possibly hear right then, and just doesn't save it.

Bitrate is the budget for what's left. A lower bitrate means it has to toss more. Music needs a big budget, lots of instruments, lots of high detail. One voice is easy. A single steady voice can sound clean at a fraction of what a song needs.

Then there's constant versus variable. Constant spends the same budget every second. Variable saves on easy stretches, like silence, and spends more on busy ones.

And here's the catch. M.P.3 only works once. Encode an M.P.3 into another M.P.3, and the second pass makes its own guesses on top of the first one's damage. Every round, it gets a little more smeared. That's why you always encode straight from the raw P.C.M. Start from the full version, and only throw stuff away once.
