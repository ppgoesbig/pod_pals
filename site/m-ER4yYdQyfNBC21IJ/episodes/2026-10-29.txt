Okay, something you've been learning. Remember picking this show's voice off that leaderboard? You already know a hundred-point gap means the higher voice should win about two times out of three. Here's how the scores actually move.

Every matchup starts with a prediction. Before a vote, the system works out how likely each voice is to win, based on the gap between their ratings. Then the vote comes in, and each voice's score moves by the difference between what happened and what was expected.

So surprise is what moves the needle. If two voices are dead even and one wins, it was a coin flip, so the winner gains a fair chunk and the loser drops the same amount. If a heavy favorite wins, that's what everyone expected, and it barely moves. But if an underdog wins, it was a real surprise, and both scores swing hard.

How hard is set by one number, usually called K. A big K means scores jump around fast. A small K means they settle slowly. Points don't appear out of nowhere either. Whatever one voice gains, the other loses.

There's a catch with plain Elo. The order of the votes matters, so a new voice that gets lucky early can sit higher than it should. That's why a lot of arenas now look at all the votes together and fit one set of scores at the end. They also show a margin around each number. When two voices sit inside each other's margin, the leaderboard can't really tell them apart. So when a new voice lands at the top by a few points, it's basically a tie.
