Okay, something new today, and it's one for the run you're probably on. Why intervals tend to come in fours.

The four by four setup comes out of research in Norway. Four minutes hard, about three minutes easy, four times. In one well-known study, people doing it for eight weeks noticeably raised their top-end fitness. People doing the same total work at a steady, moderate pace barely moved it.

The reason is how your heart ramps up. When you start a hard effort, your heart rate doesn't jump straight to the top. It climbs for a minute or two. So a short sprint ends before your heart ever gets there. Four minutes is long enough to spend a couple of those minutes close to your max.

That's the zone that matters. Near max, your heart is pushing out the most blood it can with every single beat. Spend enough time there, over and over, and the heart adapts. It gets stronger and pumps more per beat. That's a big part of what fitness people mean by a bigger engine.

The easy jog in between is clever too. It's long enough to let your legs recover, so the next round can be hard again. But it's short enough that your heart rate never drops all the way down. So the next rep gets you back up to the top faster, and you get more good minutes out of the same workout.

That's also why the rest of the week is supposed to be easy. The hard days only pay off if you show up to them fresh.
