Okay, something you've been learning. Remember planning that winter trip to the Midwest? Here's why the middle of the country gets so much colder than New York, even at about the same latitude.

It comes down to water being a heat battery. Water takes about four times as much energy to warm up as dry ground does. And the ocean mixes, so summer heat gets stirred down hundreds of feet. Land only warms the top few inches. So in winter, the ground gives up its heat in a few weeks, while the ocean keeps leaking summer warmth for months.

That's the difference between a maritime climate and a continental one. Near a big ocean, seasons are softened. Far inland, there's nothing to cushion them, so summers get hot and winters get brutal. A typical January day in Minneapolis averages somewhere in the teens. In New York it's right around freezing.

Then there's the shape of the map. The Rockies run north to south, so nothing blocks Arctic air sliding down from Canada into the Plains. It just pours straight in.
