Okay, something you've been learning. Remember finding out that Gemini's voice only hands back raw PCM? You know PCM is just a long stream of numbers. Here's how MP3 makes that stream so much smaller.

Start with the size. A voice file like the memo's, in raw PCM, runs roughly twenty-four thousand numbers every second. That's close to four hundred kilobits a second. A typical podcast MP3 is maybe sixty-four. So MP3 is throwing away most of the data, and you barely hear the difference.

The trick is that it throws away what your ears would miss anyway. MP3 chops the sound into tiny slices of time, then splits each slice into lots of frequency bands, low to high. Then it runs a model of human hearing over those bands.

That model knows two things. First, some sounds are just too quiet at their pitch for you to hear, so they get dropped. Second, and this is the clever one, a loud sound hides quieter sounds near it in pitch, and even for a split second after it. That's called masking. If a loud vowel is covering a faint hiss right next to it, MP3 spends almost no data on the hiss.

What's left gets stored more roughly, with fewer bits where the error won't show. That's why it's called lossy. Once those details are gone, they're gone.

That's the reason a good pipeline keeps the raw PCM for all the editing and loudness work, and only turns it into MP3 once, at the very end. Encode an MP3, edit it, and encode again, and each round loses a little more.
