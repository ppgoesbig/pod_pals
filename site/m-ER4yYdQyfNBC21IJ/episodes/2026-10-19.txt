Here's where the word O.K. came from. In the late eighteen-thirties, Boston newspapers had a goofy fad. Writers would abbreviate phrases as if they were misspelled on purpose. So "oll wright" became O.W., and "oll korrect," a joke spelling of all correct, became O.K. In March of eighteen thirty-nine, the Boston Morning Post printed O.K. in exactly that sense, and that's the earliest known use. Most of those jokes died out quickly. O.K. survived because of a presidential campaign. In eighteen-forty, Martin Van Buren was running for reelection, and his nickname was Old Kinderhook, after his hometown in upstate New York. His supporters formed O.K. Clubs, and the letters were suddenly everywhere. The joke spelling and the campaign slogan reinforced each other, and the word stuck. Today it's one of the most widely understood words on the planet. And it started as a newspaper gag about bad spelling.
