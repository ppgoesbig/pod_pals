Okay, something you've been learning. Remember when Gemini's voice only handed back raw PCM? Here's what that means.

PCM is sound written down as plain numbers. A microphone's signal gets measured over and over, and each measurement becomes one number. For the memo's pipeline, that's twenty-four thousand measurements every second, each one sixteen bits, in mono.

The catch is that raw PCM has no label. It's just a long stream of numbers, with nothing saying how fast to play them or how big each one is. That's why the render script has to spell it out, sixteen bit, twenty-four thousand, one channel. A WAV file is basically the same PCM with a tiny header on top that carries that label.

MP3 is a different idea. It doesn't store the wave. It stores a recipe for something that sounds close. It breaks the sound into frequencies, then leans on a trick of hearing called masking. A loud sound hides quieter sounds near it in pitch, so the encoder just throws those away.

The payoff is size. Raw, the memo runs about three hundred eighty kilobits a second. The MP3 goes out at ninety-six. So a five minute episode shrinks from around fourteen megabytes to under four and you can't hear the difference.
