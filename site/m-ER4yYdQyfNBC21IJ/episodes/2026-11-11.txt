Okay, something you've been learning. Remember building the memo feed, and how an app only grabs the M.P.3 when the feed says there's a new episode? Here's what happens next. How your phone actually pulls down that file, and why you can skip ahead before it's done.

Inside each episode in the feed there's the enclosure. It holds three things. The link to the file, what kind of file it is, and its length in bytes. That length isn't decoration. It tells the app how big the download is before it starts, so it can show progress and check it got the whole thing.

Now the clever part. When your app asks for the file, it doesn't have to ask for all of it. It can say, just send me bytes one million through two million. That's called a range request. The server sends back only that slice.

That's how skipping works. Jump to minute three, and the app does quick math. Bytes per second, times the number of seconds, roughly where minute three lives in the file. It asks for that slice and starts playing from there, without downloading the first three minutes.

It's also how a download survives a dead zone. Lose signal halfway down the block, and the app doesn't start over. It asks for the rest, starting at the byte where it stopped.

The catch is the server has to support it. Most static hosting, including the kind serving this memo, does it out of the box. A server that can't would force every skip to wait, and every dropped download to start from zero.
