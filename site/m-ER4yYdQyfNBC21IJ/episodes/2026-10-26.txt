Okay, something you've been learning. Remember setting up the memo's feed on a private path? Here's how that kind of lock works, and where it stops working.

An unguessable URL is a link with a long random string in it. There's no password. The link itself is the key. Whoever has it gets in, and whoever doesn't can't find it.

The guessing part is solid. A random string of thirty-two characters has more possible values than there are grains of sand on Earth, many times over. Nobody's stumbling onto it by trying combinations.

The weak spot is that links travel. They get pasted in a chat, and the chat app fetches a preview. They sit in browser history and in server logs. Podcast apps often sync your feeds to their own servers. And if the link is ever written somewhere public, it's not a secret anymore. That's the whole risk. Not that someone guesses it, but that it leaks.

The other limit is that you can't take the key back from one person. If a link leaks, the only fix is moving to a new random path, and then everyone who should have it needs the new one.

So it's a good fit for stuff that'd be mildly awkward to have out there, like a daily memo. It's not the right lock for anything you'd really mind someone reading.
