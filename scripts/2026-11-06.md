# Learning
Okay, something new today, and it's one from your corner. How A.I. image generators actually make a picture.

Most of them are what's called diffusion models. The training starts backwards. You take a real photo and add a little random noise, like film grain. Then a little more. Keep going, and after enough steps it's pure static. No photo left.

The model's whole job is to learn one small move. Look at a noisy image and guess what noise was added, so you can take a bit of it away. That's it. It practices that on millions of images, at every level of noise.

Making a new picture runs it forward from nothing. Start with pure static. Ask the model to remove a little noise. Then a little more. Over a few dozen steps, shapes show up, then edges, then texture, until there's an image. The prompt steers each step. At every pass, the model leans the cleanup toward whatever matches your words.

That's also why the same prompt gives you a different picture each time. The starting static is random. A different patch of noise grows into a different image, a bit like how the same scene photographed through different grain would never come out identical.

And it explains the trouble with keeping a product consistent across edits. Each image is grown fresh from noise, so nothing forces the same logo to come back exactly. Tools that do it well have to feed the model a reference picture at every step.
