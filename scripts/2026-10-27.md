# Learning
Okay, something new today, from lighting. It's called the inverse square law, and it explains a lot of what you already do by feel.

Light spreads out as it travels. So when you double the distance between a light and your subject, the light doesn't drop by half. It drops to a quarter. Triple the distance, and you get a ninth.

That's why moving a light just a little can change your exposure a lot when it's close. Going from one foot to two feet costs you two full stops. But going from ten feet to eleven barely registers.

Here's the useful part. Falloff matters too, not just brightness. With a light very close, the front of a product can be bright while the back goes dark fast, because the back is relatively much farther away. Pull the light way back, turn the power up, and the whole object lights more evenly, front to back.

So it's a choice you can make on purpose. Close light gives you drama and a fast fade into shadow. Far light gives you evenness. One catch. A light that's closer also looks bigger to the subject, so it gets softer too. Pulling it back buys evenness but makes it a little harder.

Next time a piece of silver looks bright on one end and muddy on the other, try backing the light off before you add a second one.
