# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember picking this show's voice off that leaderboard? You already know how the scores move, and that close scores are basically ties. Here's the next layer. What a blind vote actually measures, and what it misses.

Start with order. In any listen-and-compare test, people lean a little toward one slot, often whichever clip they heard last, since it's freshest. That's why good arenas shuffle which voice plays first. Over thousands of votes, the bias cancels out.

Then loudness, and this ties right into your minus sixteen. If one clip is even slightly louder, most people hear it as better. Not clearer, just better. Audio folks call it the louder-is-better trap. So a fair comparison matches the loudness first, the same way your pipeline evens out every episode.

The bigger catch is length. Those votes happen on short clips, a sentence or two. So the leaderboard is really ranking first impressions. A voice can be dazzling for ten seconds and wearing by minute four. A little too bright, a pet rhythm that keeps coming back. Five minutes on a run is a totally different test.

And last, it's the crowd's taste, not yours. The top voice is the one most voters liked, on lines somebody else picked.

So the leaderboard is a great shortlist. The real test is the one you're running right now, listening to a whole episode while you move. If anything about this voice has started to bug you, that's the data that counts.
