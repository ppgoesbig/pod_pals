# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember building the memo's feed? You already know each episode gets its own I.D. Here's what else is going on when an app checks for new ones.

The feed is just one text file. At the top, it describes the show. Then there's an entry for each episode. Every entry has a piece called the enclosure, and that's where the audio actually lives. It holds three things. The link to the file, its size, and what kind of file it is, like an MP3. The app reads that before downloading anything, so it knows how much space it needs and whether it can play it.

The polling part is clever. Apps don't get pinged when you publish. They come and ask, usually every hour or so. Most apps don't even run that check from your phone. A server checks the feed for everyone, then tells your phone there's something new. If thousands of people subscribe to a show, the host might get one visit instead of thousands.

And they try not to waste the trip. When the app asks for the feed, it says, I last saw a version from this time, with this tag on it. If nothing's changed, the server answers with a tiny reply that basically says, same as before. No file gets sent at all. Only when the feed is different does the full thing come down.

That's why a new episode can take a little while to show up. Your push lands, the feed updates, and then it waits for the next time the app comes asking.
