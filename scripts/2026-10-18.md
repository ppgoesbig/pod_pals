# One thing to learn
Here's how a railroad schedule gave us time zones. Before the eighteen-eighties, every American town set its clocks by the sun. Noon was whenever the sun was highest over that particular town, so when it was noon in New York, it was a few minutes earlier in Philadelphia and a few minutes later in Boston. For people on foot, that was fine. For railroads, it was chaos. Companies ran on dozens of different local times, and timetables were a nightmare to read and dangerous to get wrong. So on November eighteenth, eighteen eighty-three, the railroads in the United States and Canada simply agreed to switch to a handful of standard zones. Stations reset their clocks at noon, and in many towns the hands jumped or stopped for a few minutes. People later called it the day of two noons. Here's the surprising part. The government didn't make those zones official law until nineteen eighteen. For thirty-five years, the country basically ran on railroad time because the trains said so.
