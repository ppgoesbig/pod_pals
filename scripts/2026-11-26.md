# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember looking for rain gear for biking? Here's how a jacket can keep rain out and still let sweat escape.

The classic trick is a thin membrane with billions of tiny pores. Each pore is something like twenty thousand times smaller than a raindrop, so liquid water can't push through. But it's hundreds of times bigger than a single water vapor molecule. So sweat, as vapor, can slip out, while rain stays outside. Gore-Tex is the famous one.

There's a second kind with no holes at all. It's a solid film that kind of soaks up water molecules on the warm side and passes them through, one at a time, to the cool side. Slower to start, but tougher.

Here's the part people miss. Breathing isn't a pump. Vapor only moves from warm and humid to cool and dry. So on a cold dry day, it works great. On a warm muggy rainy day, there's barely any difference between inside and out, and it hardly breathes at all. That's why you can feel soaked in a waterproof jacket. It's often your own sweat, not a leak.

Then there's the outside coating. The face fabric gets a water-repellent finish so rain beads up and rolls off. When that wears off, the fabric soaks through, which is called wetting out. The membrane's still waterproof, but now there's a cold wet layer over it, and breathing basically stops. A wash and a little heat, like a dryer on low, often brings the beading back.

And on a bike, you're making way more sweat than any membrane can move. So vents under the arms do more than the fanciest fabric.
