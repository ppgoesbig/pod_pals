# One thing to learn
Here's why the Martian moon Phobos is doomed. Phobos orbits incredibly close to Mars, only about six thousand kilometers above the surface. It's so close that it goes all the way around the planet in about seven and a half hours, faster than Mars itself spins. That creates a strange situation. The tidal bulge that Phobos raises on Mars lags behind it, and that bulge gently tugs Phobos backward, draining its orbital energy. So instead of drifting away the way our Moon slowly drifts away from Earth, Phobos is spiraling inward, by a couple of meters every century. That sounds tiny, but it adds up. In something like thirty to fifty million years, Mars's gravity will either pull Phobos apart into a ring of rubble, or it'll crash into the surface. Some scientists even think the long grooves on Phobos are the first stretch marks of that slow breakup. So the moon Japan just sent a probe to is a moon on a countdown.
