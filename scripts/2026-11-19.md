# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember building the memo feed? You know it's a file your podcast app checks, with one entry per episode. Today's how the app decides what's new without wasting anyone's time.

Every episode entry carries a tag called the enclosure. That's the actual audio, with three things. Where the file lives, how big it is in bytes, and what kind of file it is. The size lets the app show a download estimate before it grabs anything.

Then there's the G.U.I.D., the episode's permanent name tag. The app doesn't judge newness by the title or the date. It just asks, have I seen this tag before? So if a tag ever changes, even on an old episode, the app thinks it's brand new and downloads it again. That's how people end up with ten copies of the same show. Set it once and never touch it.

Now the checking. Your app doesn't get told when there's a new episode. It just asks the feed every so often, anywhere from every few minutes to every few hours, depending on the app. That's called polling.

And here's the polite part. Most apps don't download the whole feed every time. They send a little note with the request, saying here's the version I last saw. If nothing changed, the server answers with a tiny reply that just means nope, same as before. No file sent at all. That's why thousands of apps can check a feed all day without melting the server. And it's why a new memo can take a little while to show up. Your app just hasn't asked yet.
