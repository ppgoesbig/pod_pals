# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember setting up the memo's audio, and the target of about minus sixteen LUFS? Here's what that number is actually measuring.

LUFS stands for loudness units relative to full scale. Full scale is the loudest a digital file can go. So every reading is a negative number, counting down from that ceiling. Minus sixteen is louder than minus twenty.

The clever part is that it tries to measure loudness the way your ear does, not the way a meter does. Your ears aren't equally sensitive to every pitch. Deep bass takes a lot of energy to sound loud, while the middle range, where voices live, sounds loud with much less. So the measurement filters the audio first. It turns down the very low end and gives a small boost up top, roughly how hearing works.

Then it skips the quiet parts. Gaps between sentences and near-silence get left out of the average, so a pause-heavy episode doesn't read as quieter than it sounds.

What's left is averaged across the whole episode into one number. That's why it's called integrated loudness.

Why minus sixteen? It's the common podcast target, loud enough to hear over a bus or a bike lane on a phone, with room left so speech doesn't sound squashed. Music apps like Spotify normalize a bit louder, around minus fourteen. And there's one more guardrail. The tallest peaks get kept about one decibel under the ceiling, because turning audio into MP3 can push peaks a little higher than they were.
