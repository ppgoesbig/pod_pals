# Learning
Okay, something you've been learning. Remember setting the memo's audio to that target loudness? You already know how loudness gets measured, and why there's a ceiling on peaks. Today's the other half. Why the loud and quiet parts inside one episode matter more than the overall number, especially for you, right now, with one earbud in.

There's a measurement for that spread, called loudness range. It's the gap between the quieter stretches and the louder stretches of a recording, in the same units as the loudness target. A film can swing from whispers to explosions. On a street, the whispers vanish under a bus, so you turn it up, and then the explosion hits.

For listening on the move, you want a narrow range. Everything sits in a tight band, so one volume setting works the whole way through.

The tool that does that is a compressor. You set a threshold, and anything louder than it gets turned down by a ratio. Two to one means for every two steps over the line, only one comes through. Then you raise the whole thing back up, so the quiet parts end up closer to the loud ones.

Here's the nice part for the memo. A T.T.S. voice is already very even. No one leans away from the mic or laughs too loud. So it barely needs compressing, which is part of why it's easy to hear over traffic. Normalization just sets the overall level, and the voice was steady to begin with.
